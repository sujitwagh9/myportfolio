## Problem

A classifier that only says "pneumonia: yes" isn't much use in practice. To be worth anything as a screening aid, a result has to show **where** the model looked, **how confident** it is, and come in a form that can be passed on for clinical review.

## What it does

- **Upload** a frontal chest X-ray (PNG or JPEG, up to 10 MB). You can also paste one in.
- **Classify:** an EfficientNetB0 network trained on 5,856 labelled chest X-rays estimates the probability of pneumonia against a 0.5 decision threshold.
- **Explain:** Grad-CAM highlights the lung regions that most influenced the decision. You can view the original image, the heatmap, a blend, or a side-by-side compare slider, with zoom and pan.
- **Report:** a structured screening note is drafted by Gemini for clinician review, then bundled with the images into a downloadable PDF.
- **Be honest about uncertainty:** results near the threshold are flagged as borderline and inconclusive.
- **Privacy:** images are processed on the server only, never used for training, and auto-deleted after 60 minutes.

## How it works

Each analysis runs as a background job with live progress: **waiting in queue → classifying and computing Grad-CAM → drafting the written report → building the PDF**. The whole pipeline takes roughly 10–40 seconds.

## Demo

The walkthrough above was recorded from the app running locally with two chest X-rays:

- The **first** came back at a pneumonia probability of 0.531. The app flags this as pneumonia but warns it's borderline, which is exactly the kind of case where a heatmap and a clinician matter.
- The **second** came back at 0.350, so no pneumonia detected, with 65% confidence.

## Stack

Python · Flask · TensorFlow · EfficientNetB0 · Grad-CAM · Gemini API

## Outcome

A research prototype that turns a single prediction into something a reviewer can check: a probability, a confidence, a heatmap and a written report, all in one PDF. It's built for research and education only, and it's not a medical device.
